
## Contrastive Learning Notes

- Technique in deep learning falling under representation learning.
- Better representations allow to better learn a domain, and better understand the underlying structure of the data.
- Can be used in supervised, semi-supervised, and self-supervised learning scenarios.
- Instance discrimination is a common approach in contrastive learning
- Unsupervised/Semi-supervised contrastive learning uses augmentation to create positive pairs from the same instance and negative pairs from different instances.
- The objective is to bring representations of positive pairs closer while pushing representations of negative pairs apart in the embedding space.
- The hope is representations learned are generalizable and can be used across different tasks and domains.
- Contrastive learning helps us to learn compact representations, and in addition the ability to learn from unlabeled data effectively.
- Popular contrastive learning frameworks include SimCLR and CLIP.
- Common loss functions used in contrastive learning include contrastive loss, Triplet loss, and InfoNCE loss.
